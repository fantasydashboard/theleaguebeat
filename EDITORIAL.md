# Ultimate Fantasy Dashboard — Editorial Voice Manifesto

The brand voice that all auto-generated copy must inherit. If a sentence violates a rule here, it doesn't ship. This file is the seed for ~2,000 variant strings across the editorial system — start every library file by re-reading this.

## What it sounds like

Magazine column, not stat dump. ESPN's Sunday morning recap, not a SportsCenter highlight. League group chat at 11pm, not corporate communications. Confident, never timid.

The reader is a commissioner who's been in the league for years. Don't explain the game to them. Tell them what just happened in a way that earns the click and starts the conversation.

## The seven rules

1. **Active voice, present tense.** "Bullpen Theology took the throne." not "The throne was taken by Bullpen Theology" or "Bullpen Theology has taken the throne over the past few weeks."

2. **Specific numbers anchor every sentence.** A sentence without a number reads as filler. Win counts, ranks, point margins, streak lengths, week numbers — drop them in. "Three straight wins" beats "a hot streak."

3. **Sentence-length variety.** Most sentences are short. Some are medium. A few are longer. The rhythm IS the voice. Avoid uniform 12-word sentences in a row — it reads as templated.

4. **Last names only after first mention.** "Bobby Witt Jr. dropped three." then "Witt's been the engine for weeks." Mascot logos carry first-name identity; copy doesn't need to repeat.

5. **Verbs do work. Adjectives do not.** "Climbed" not "successfully climbed." "Owns" not "completely owns." "Bleeds" not "really bleeds." If you can delete an adjective without losing meaning, delete it. (Exception: descriptors that carry information — "back-to-back champ," "first-round bust.")

6. **The headline IS the trash talk.** The Barlow Black hero text is the part that gets screenshotted and dropped into a chat. Write it like someone is going to forward it. "The dynasty falls." is forwardable. "Power rankings updated" is not.

7. **Wayfinding only — never address the reader as "you."** "Mound Visitors clawed back to .500" not "Your team clawed back to .500." The yellow row tint is the only "you" cue. Editorial copy is third-person across the board.

## Sentence-length distribution (target)

- 70% under 10 words
- 25% 10-20 words
- 5% 20-30 words (used sparingly, only for setup/analytical context)

Anything 30+ words gets cut. Two short sentences always beat one long one.

## Verb register (the working vocabulary)

These verbs do most of the lifting. Use them.

**Movement:** took, climbed, fell, dropped, slipped, surged, collapsed, leapfrogged, dethroned, displaced, overtook, ascended, swept

**State:** owns, holds, leads, bleeds, punts, coasts, struggles, hangs on, sits, rides

**Performance:** dropped (a number), put up, hung (a score), padded, sealed, cooked, vanished, exploded, fizzled, padded, locked, flipped

**Streaks:** broke (a streak), extended, snapped, ended

If a verb feels like it could appear in a SaaS dashboard ("achieved," "delivered," "demonstrated"), don't use it.

## What never appears

- **Em dashes** (—). Use commas, colons, periods, parentheses, or semicolons.
- **Double-hyphen em dash substitutes** (--).
- **Emojis** in any copy. Mascot images do that work.
- **Exclamation points.** Almost never. The headline weight does the emphasis.
- **Filler intensifiers:** "incredible," "amazing," "epic," "absolutely," "literally," "actually," "really," "very," "truly."
- **Sports-broadcast clichés:** "left it all on the field," "did the little things," "wanted it more," "showed up when it mattered."
- **Corporate hedge phrases:** "appears to have," "looks like," "seems," "potentially."
- **Second-person "you"** in editorial copy (only in wayfinding tags like "Your team" pill labels).
- **Generic descriptors:** "great game," "strong performance," "solid week," "tough loss." Be specific or delete.
- **Question-mark headlines.** Headlines are statements. "Is Bullpen Theology unstoppable?" never; "Bullpen Theology might be unstoppable." instead, OR write something else.

## 15 canonical sentences (the voice in practice)

These are the reference set. New copy should sound like it belongs in this list.

1. **The dynasty falls.**
2. **Bullpen Theology just took the throne.**
3. **Three straight wins. 132.4 points per game. The conversation just changed.**
4. **Closer's Therapy is bleeding saves. The bullpen has been quiet for three weeks.**
5. **Eight spots since week 1. Doubles Down is the climber of the year.**
6. **Skubal dropped twelve. The K race is locked.**
7. **Witt went off. Bullpen Theology now leads HR over Doubles Down.**
8. **Nabers vanished. Three catches on seven targets.**
9. **Reign Delay needed a real game. They got eighty-four.**
10. **The Wagon fell to .500 for the first time all year.**
11. **Closer's Therapy went from #1 to #6. The dynasty has cracked.**
12. **By the Numbers held the line. Five straight weeks above the bubble.**
13. **Auto-Draft Allstars: five-game losing streak. The basement is calling.**
14. **First meeting since 2024. Bullpen Theology won that one by a category.**
15. **One more save Friday or the SV lead flips. Closer's Therapy is on notice.**

## The two failure modes to watch for

**1. "It reads fine but feels templated."**
This happens when every sentence has the same shape (subject-verb-object, ~10 words). Fix by varying rhythm: drop a 3-word fragment in. Open with a number. Lead with a verb. Trail off with a kicker.

**2. "It sounds like a sportswriter trying too hard."**
This happens when verbs get fancy ("decimated," "obliterated," "annihilated") or adjectives pile on ("absolutely scorching white-hot offense"). Fix by stripping back to one strong verb and a specific number. Trust the data to carry the weight.

## When a story has no drama

Sometimes the data doesn't produce a hero moment. Week 3 of a new league. A matchup decided by 1 cat with nothing on the line. Don't manufacture stakes that aren't there. The copy gets quieter, more matter-of-fact. "Quality Start held serve. Five-five with one to play." is honest and still on-brand.

The voice contracts when there's no story. It doesn't disappear.
